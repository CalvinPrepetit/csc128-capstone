I would like to continue working with the auto shop theme I have been keeping throughout the module assignments this semester. The purpose of the bot will be to assist customers with explaining their vehicle issue, setting up a service visit, and creating an appointment or service request ticket. I am proposing this because it has real world practicality, fits the intent of the assignments well, and from a development perspective it helps me stay aligned with the logic I have already been building instead of switching into a completely new concept area.

The bot will have four main intents: service issue triage, appointment setting, service request ticket creation, and technician intake summary with confirmation. Service issue triage will be the part where the bot helps break down what is going on with the vehicle, asks follow-up questions when useful, and determines where the issue best aligns based on available departments such as electrical, drivability, interior, exterior, and maintenance. Appointment setting will collect the appointment details needed for the service visit. Service request ticket creation will connect the customer’s issue details to the appointment request. The technician intake summary and confirmation intent will read back the customer’s issue details, likely service area or areas, and appointment details before the request is committed.

The bot will collect entities such as the type of service being requested, the customer’s issue details, what the customer expects to have completed, the department selection, appointment day and time, vehicle count if needed, and a service summary for the technician. Exact values like appointment day, appointment time, vehicle count, and confirmation details will be handled with deterministic code because I do not want the model guessing values that need to be predictable and testable.

The language model will be used to help the customer better explain their service needs in a way that is clear and digestible for the shop and assigned technician. For example, if a customer describes the issue in a messy or uncertain way, the model can ask focused follow-up questions and help turn that into a cleaner service note without pretending to diagnose the vehicle with certainty.

For the tool and grounding requirement, I plan to use both in a controlled way. The bot will call local Python helper functions for things that need exact outputs, such as mock appointment slots, stored appointment details, ticket creation, and confirmation information. It will also use a small document set for stable shop information, such as department descriptions, drop-off instructions, and what customers should bring to a service visit. For department matching, the language model can use the department descriptions to interpret messy customer wording, including cases where more than one department may apply. However, the final department selection must still be limited to the approved shop departments and will be confirmed before the request is committed.

The bot will refuse anything outside the service advisor scope. That includes exact repair prices, warranty decisions, insurance claims, recall lookups, or advice that replaces a qualified technician. If something is outside scope, it will explain what it can help with instead and direct the customer to the shop, manufacturer, insurance provider, or qualified mechanic as appropriate.

Overall, the goal is to keep the scope realistic while building a bot that feels useful for a real auto shop service intake process.
